# Review of the simulation approach and implementation (2026-11-01)

Scope: draft v0.2 (`docs/draft_v02_ddtf_multimodal_hyperscanning_simulations.md`), `docs/methodology_multimodal_ddtf_pipeline.md`,
`src/sim_*.py`, `src/design.py`, the estimators in `src/sim_estimation.py` / `src/mtmvar.py`, S01–S11, and the full dDTF and GPDC runs
(`simulations_multimodal_MVAR/dDTF/Results/`, commit 4d51a85). Two independent reviews (implementation, methodology) were run and their
main claims re-checked against the stored results; checks marked **[re-checked]** were reproduced, **[unverified]** were not.

## Verdict

The code is correct: no indexing, orientation, sign or normalisation bugs in the estimators, generators or surrogate construction.
The problems are statistical and in the design/claims:

1. dDTF with the surrogate null does not test individual edges (reverse and indirect edges are detected); GPDC does.
2. Within-person edges cannot be tested with person-swapping surrogates at all.
3. The study-level Wilcoxon test is miscalibrated (liberal for dDTF, conservative for GPDC); the dyad-level z > 1.96 too.
4. The group-contrast test (median difference, permutation) caps power at about 0.55 with 5 vs 5 dyads.
5. Several draft claims (selectivity of A vs A′, S02 phase-path collapse, S05 conditioning) are not supported by what the simulations do.

## Implementation: verified correct

- `detrend_windows` (closed form) equals `scipy.signal.detrend` (max error 1.3e-15).
- `ar_coeff` / `count_corr` recover a known VAR on a long series (planted 0.4 → 0.401, matches OLS within 4e-5); the planted edge lands at `[target, source]`.
- Fast estimators equal the repository estimators (`tests/test_sim_estimation.py`, `tests/test_ddtf_theory.py`, 7/7 pass).
- Partial coherence from S⁻¹ = Aᴴ Σ⁻¹ A; on true cascade coefficients the indirect dDTF is < 1e-12.
- Normalisations: DTF rows sum to 1, GPDC columns (squared) sum to 1 with σ of the target row (Baccalá 2007), ffDTF over the given grid (same for real and surrogate).
- AIC/HQ/SC formulas standard; `build_coupling` writes `[target, source, lag]`; true models stable (max root 0.92).
- Surrogates: child rows from dyad b, caregiver rows from dyad c ≠ b; 'exclude' drops every pair containing either member;
  within-person structure and the time-aligned stimulus are kept, cross-person coupling is broken.
- Seeds are independent per (cell, replicate); the dDTF and GPDC runs are paired.

## Major issues

### 1. dDTF + surrogate null detects dependence between persons, not edges
dDTF = ffDTF · |κ| and |κ| (partial coherence) is symmetric. A true edge j→i makes |κ| large in real dyads and ≈0 in surrogates, so the reverse
dDTF (ffDTF finite-sample floor × large |κ|) is real > null. On a cascade, the indirect ffDTF is large and the biased |κ| floor is the same in real
and null data, so again real > null. Results **[re-checked]**: reverse detection S08 baseline dDTF 0.68, ffDTF/DTF 0.17, GPDC 0.055; absent indirect
edge S05 (gain 0.4) dDTF/ffDTF/DTF 1.00, GPDC 0.025; S11 dDTF 1.00, GPDC 0.00. This is a property of the estimator exposed by the test, not a
design artefact (the HANDOFF diagnosis agrees: |κ| estimate decays only as 1/√n, longer recordings make it worse).
**Consequence:** with dDTF the paper cannot claim "direct", "unidirectional" or "bidirectional" edges; direction dominance (forward > reverse) is still valid.
**Options:** GPDC (or a test on A(f) / conditional time-domain GC) as the primary edge-wise statistic; dDTF only as a dominance contrast
(Δfwd − Δrev) or descriptive measure; rewrite §2.1 and §3.5 ("suppresses edges that are only indirect").

### 2. Within-person edges are untestable with this null
Surrogates keep each person's nodes together, so real and null share the within-person structure. S05 **[re-checked]**: the planted
child:ROI→child:HRV edge has power 0.00 with every estimator; the common-driver "absent edge" is within the caregiver (cg:ROI→cg:HRV), so FPR is 0
for every method including bivariate — S05 does not test conditioning, and its "planted power ≥ 0.8" criterion fails by construction.
The draft (§8.1) and the methodology doc ("intrabrain", brain–heart) promise within-person edges.
**Fix:** restrict surrogate inference to cross-person edges, or give within-person edges another null (per-person time-shift/IAAFT, parametric);
redesign S05 with an absent *cross-person* edge through a cascade, a common driver of both persons, and an omitted-driver arm (as the draft describes).

### 3. Study-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test is miscalibrated
The signed-rank test assumes Δ symmetric about 0; null Δ has median 0 but is skewed. From the stored S03 null z (N = 50, 300 s) **[re-checked]**:
dDTF skew +0.46 → Wilcoxon FPR 0.082 vs sign test 0.030; GPDC skew −0.28 → 0.033 vs 0.034. S03's own study FPR rises with N for dDTF
(0.06 → 0.08) and falls for GPDC (0.045 → 0.034); without Box-Cox dDTF reaches 0.23 (S08). The real pipeline's default is no Box-Cox
(`box_cox_lambda = -1`), so as configured it would be strongly liberal. Power comparisons of dDTF vs GPDC are confounded by this.
**Fix:** an exact pairing-permutation test (real dyads vs re-paired pseudo-dyads, mean or median), or a sign test; validity should not depend on Box-Cox.

### 4. Dyad-level z > 1.96 is not calibrated
One-sided nominal 0.025; observed dyad FPR in S03 dDTF ≈ 0.04, GPDC ≈ 0.012 (P(z > 1.96) 0.042 / 0.012 **[re-checked]**). The MAD-based z
assumes a symmetric null. **Fix:** empirical p = (r + 1)/(M + 1) from the rank among surrogates.

### 5. Group contrast with small groups: power capped by the median statistic
S04 design B, N = 10 (5 vs 5): power plateaus at ≈ 0.55 for gains 0.2–0.5 even at 300 s. Cause **[re-checked]**: the group p-values cluster around
0.05 (median 0.050, 75th percentile 0.056) even when the groups are separated. With the difference of medians of 5 values, about half of all splits
reach a difference as large as the observed one, so p cannot go far below ≈ 0.05. **Fix:** mean difference or rank-sum statistic (exact minimum p for 5 vs 5
= 2/252); then re-run S04 B and S07. The "N ≥ 25 for group contrasts" conclusion in HANDOFF rests partly on this artefact.

### 6. Exchangeability: dyad-level shared traits inflate the FPR
The null is "independent persons given the film", not "no interaction". Where real pairs share a trait without interacting, surrogates do not:
S07 one-group pooled null dDTF FPR up to 0.16 (stimulus gain shared by both members), S09 truth 'none' at gap 0: reverse rate dDTF 0.16, GPDC 0.07
(matched carrier frequencies) **[re-checked]**. Realistic counterparts: shared engagement, session, breathing rate. **Fix:** state the null hypothesis
formally (§3.6); add a simulation with dyad-correlated nuisance traits; consider matched/stratified surrogates.

### 7. Ground truth is defined in the generator, not for the observed 4-node process
Level 1 is a correctly specified Gaussian VAR (true order ≤ 3, fitted p = 4, no observation noise, no hidden node): the estimator is tested inside
its own model class. In Level 2 (envelopes, observation noise, filtering, downsampling) the observed process is VARMA/nonlinear, and real
reverse/indirect Granger links appear that were never planted (e.g. S09 pulling: reverse 1.00 dDTF, 0.94 GPDC). Counting them as false positives
mixes estimator error with a correct Granger answer. **Fix:** score against the population GC/GPDC of the observed process (analytic or a very long run);
add observation noise and an unobserved node to Level 1.

### 8. Claims not supported
- **S10 / Table 1:** A detects 'amp_to_eeg' (up to 0.97) and A′ detects 'linear' (up to 1.00); the A-vs-A′ selectivity premise fails. Drop the
  claim that the representation identifies coupling type.
- **S02:** under amplitude truth the phase path equals truth 'none', so the "phase path drops ≥ 50%" criterion measures drift of the ffDTF normalisation,
  not loss of coupling (GPDC does not drop); phase truth uses fixed K, so locking is lost beyond ≈ 1 Hz detuning; no surrogate test; settings differ from
  the pipeline (2 Hz, global AIC fit, no windows, no Box-Cox). Under 'none' the dDTF phase path shows a spurious direction index of −0.57 at large gaps.
  The GPDC `phase_fixedgrid` values are on another scale (≈ 70–95 vs 0.1) — use ratios only.

## Minor issues

- **Windowed Yule-Walker on 25-sample windows** shrinks coefficients (self a1 0.70 → 0.53, lag-2 edge 0.32 → 0.235; lag-1 HRV edge inflated 0.20 → 0.24),
  so "recovery" numbers carry lag-dependent bias; biased-ACF YW is always stable, so `STABILITY_MAX_ROOT` filtering never acts.
- **"Resonant" ROI nodes** with r = 0.45–0.65 are near low-pass (r 0.45, f0 0.2 Hz: peak at 0 Hz); describe them as such.
- **S08 order variants** select p per fit, so real and surrogate fits can have different orders; selection runs on the un-detrended whole series.
- **dDTF definition:** code uses ffDTF × |κ| (not squared); Korzeniewska et al. 2003 use the squared partial coherence **[unverified, check the paper]**.
  Monotone per edge, but state the formula.
- **Monte Carlo precision:** SE of FPR 0.05 is 0.015 at 200 replicates, 0.022 at 100; the S03 band [0.03, 0.07] must hold in 18 cells and will fail by
  chance even under perfect calibration. Report pooled FPR with CIs, use one prespecified tolerance (e.g. Wilson upper bound), ≥ 1000 replicates for FPR claims.
- **Criteria:** still `[proposed]` after the full runs; some added later ("informative"); several differ from the draft (S5, S8, S3 per-dyad rule).
  Vacuous ones: S11 "lowest absent-indirect rate" passes on a 1.00 = 1.00 tie; S09 FPR checks the forward edge only; S09 "power at largest gap < gap 0"
  cannot fail when saturated. Freeze criteria with a commit hash and label changes as post hoc.
- **Draft vs code:** order (draft SC, code p = 4), band (0.15–0.5 vs 0.2–1.0 Hz in the methodology doc), Box-Cox before band-averaging in code (after in draft),
  no observation noise / SNR variation in S03/S04 (draft says so), S04 gain grid 0.1 vs 0.05, unused drift support in S08, stale Table 2 statuses.
- **Effect size:** VAR gain is not interpretable; express it as population GC/GPDC or variance explained, anchored to real-data values.
- **Multiplicity:** per-edge FPR only; with 6 edges the family-wise rate is ≈ 0.3. Simulate the BH-FDR (or max-statistic) actually used.
- **S11:** competitors fitted on the whole segment, dDTF windowed — not like-for-like.
- **Small code nits:** `lagged(x, 0)` returns an empty array (`src/sim_generators.py`); `variance_modulation` modulates the SD, not the variance;
  `represent()` docstring bands differ from the code (`src/sim_cardiac.py`).

## Missing simulations

1. Realistic IBI: beat generation (IPFM) at child and adult heart rates, ectopics + correction, interpolation (role-specific smoothing, Nyquist of the beat rate inside the RSA band).
2. Non-stationarity: drift, coupling onset, coupling in part of the film.
3. Cross-modal delay / clock offset between EEG and ECG.
4. Unequal SNR between persons/roles; child artefact bursts.
5. True order > p on short data; real/surrogate order mismatch.
6. Unequal segment lengths, missing data, group-dependent exclusion of unstable fits.
7. Respiration as an omitted common driver (the paper's main limitation).
8. Coupling in only some dyads; power under a strong common stimulus.
9. Coupling outside the analysis band or at lags > p.

## What is sound

Whole-study replication including the surrogate step; paired seeds across estimators; the within-group null (S07: pooled-null group FPR up to
0.78–0.90 when gains differ, within-group ≤ 0.05); film matching (other-film null inflates the FPR); the S06 smoothness artefact and its removal by the
surrogate correction; group-level FPR in S03 near nominal; direction accuracy ≈ 1 whenever power is high.

**Supportable now:** study-level detection that two persons are dependent (once the test is fixed), the dominant direction of cross-person coupling,
removal of stimulus and smoothness confounds by film-matched within-group nulls. **Not yet:** direct vs indirect or unidirectional coupling with dDTF,
within-person edges, coupling-type identification via A/A′, phase measures in general being biased by detuning.

## Suggested order of work

1. Replace the study-level test (pairing permutation or sign test) and the dyad-level rule (empirical p); replace the group statistic (mean / rank-sum).
2. Decide the primary edge statistic (GPDC, or dDTF only as dominance contrast); rewrite the corresponding draft sections.
3. Redesign S05 (cross-person cascade, common driver, omitted driver); restrict the null to cross-person edges.
4. Fix S02 (surrogates, gap-scaled K, pipeline settings) and revise Table 1 / S10.
5. Freeze criteria, raise S03 replicates, then re-run everything.
